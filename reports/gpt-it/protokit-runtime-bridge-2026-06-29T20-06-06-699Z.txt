Executive pitch

The Open Above is a third-person wilderness flight adventure where you live as a wild bird crossing a vast, breathing mountain world, not by chasing rings or markers, but by reading terrain, clouds, wind, weather, flock motion, animal behavior, and the shape of the land itself. You dive into valleys to gather speed, carve along ridgelines, catch lift under storm-dark clouds, perch on ancient trees, migrate with flocks, survive weather fronts, defend roosts, discover hidden ecological landmarks, and gradually master the sky as a real navigable habitat. The promise is simple: the world teaches you how to fly.

1. What the player does every minute

Launch: Start from a perch, cliff, nest, canopy branch, ruin, or moving flock.

Read: Scan horizon, clouds, tree motion, birds, rain curtains, cliffs, shadows, and water.

Commit: Pick a terrain line: ridge, valley, river, canopy corridor, cliff wall, cloud shelf.

Dive: Drop for speed, stabilize, skim terrain, avoid branches and rock faces.

Carve: Bank around ridges, roll through wind shear, hold altitude with terrain lift.

Exploit: Catch thermal, ridge lift, storm updraft, canopy vent, waterfall spray, sea breeze.

React: Weather shifts, predators appear, flock changes heading, visibility drops, stamina dips.

Navigate: Use landmarks, flock calls, sun angle, river direction, mountain silhouettes, smell cues.

Recover: Flap out of stall, shelter under cliff, perch to rest, ride flock draft, escape turbulence.

Resolve: Reach roost, discover habitat, rescue fledgling, find migration route, survive storm, claim perch.

No rings. No checkpoint race. The challenge is natural reading, embodied flight, and ecological navigation.

2. Full-game identity
Axis	Direction
Genre	Open-world bird flight simulator, ecological traversal adventure, wilderness survival-lite
Camera	Third-person over-bird follow camera, close enough to feel wing motion, wide enough to read terrain
Mood	Awe, altitude, danger, solitude, migration, storm beauty, ancient wilderness
Target player	Players who love movement mastery, nature games, atmospheric exploration, gliding, emergent systems
Player fantasy	“I am not piloting a plane. I am a living bird reading the sky.”
Core promise	Master flight by learning how weather, landforms, ecology, and flock behavior interact
Platform assumptions	Browser-first Three.js, keyboard initially, gamepad later, WebGPU/WebGL renderer path, deterministic sim core
Different from flying games	No aircraft UI, no speedometer obsession, no ring racing, no abstract courses
Different from survival games	Survival is ecological and movement-based, not inventory-grid crafting
Different from walking exploration	The main verb is reading moving air across land at speed
Nexus identity	A flagship proof that many generic NexusRealtime DSKs can compose into one expressive standalone game
3. Core design pillars

Terrain is the level designer: Ridges, valleys, cliffs, rivers, forests, canopies, and cloud decks create flight lines.

Weather is the enemy and the opportunity: Storms threaten, but lift lives inside them.

The flock is a living interface: Birds communicate safe paths, hazards, migration intent, panic, rest, and opportunity.

Movement is the progression: Better player skill plus body archetype unlocks, not stat inflation alone.

No ring-race grammar: Navigation challenges use landmarks, wind, ecology, silhouettes, calls, scent, stars, water, and memory.

The game is data-driven: The app owns Open Above data, generic kits own reusable simulation meaning.

Host is a renderer and conductor: Long-term, the host should not own bird physics, weather rules, cloud rules, flock meaning, perch rules, or objective logic.

Preserve from NexusRealtime-Experiments source inspiration

Preserve these ideas as design and architecture constraints:

Generic ProtoKit composition: The game is a composition of generic DSKs, not a bespoke monolith.

App-owned config: open-above.config.js style data ownership should carry forward into NexusEngine-TheOpenAbove.

Host owns rendering: Three.js, RAF, HUD, input plumbing, debug surface, and canvas lifecycle stay host-side.

Public kit APIs: Input maps to kit APIs, not private state mutation.

GameHost debug surface: Keep a transparent browser debug/control surface.

Direct assisted flight: Preserve immediate, readable keyboard response.

Low carve camera authority: The camera should follow and reveal, not steer the player.

Persistent target and position lag: Keep the physical feeling of a trailing camera.

No heuristic autopilot loop: The bird is assisted, not secretly flown by a pathing hack.

Canonical route convention: Make the standalone base route the canonical identity, not split V1/V2 gallery cards.

Discard or avoid

Discard harness identity: This should stop feeling like a flight harness and start feeling like a finished game slice.

Discard ring grammar: No checkpoint rings, terrain rings, arcade ring challenges, or ring objectives.

Discard tint-only worldbuilding: Landmarks need geometry, silhouettes, motion, sound, and flight utility.

Discard primitive prop clutter: Prefer fewer, authored, readable landmarks over noisy object scatter.

Discard host-owned domain rules: Do not let the route become a bird/weather/flock/objective simulator internally.

Discard V1/V2 split behavior: Successful V2 decisions should fold into the base game identity.

Discard autopilot-like camera corrections: Camera assists should be transparent and low-authority.
